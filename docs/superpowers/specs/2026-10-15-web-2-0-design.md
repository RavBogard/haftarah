# Haftarah 2.0: the engine, the reading model, and the study features

Design spec, 2026-10-15. The decisions in section 2 were made by Daniel in conversation on 2026-10-14 and 2026-10-15 and are not reopened here. The research behind them is in `docs/superpowers/research/2026-10-15-web-2-0/` (eight reports, cited by file name below). Wave 1 has its own implementation plan, `docs/superpowers/plans/2026-10-15-web-2-0-wave-1.md`; each later wave gets its own plan when it starts.

## 1. Goal and audience

Make the weekly haftarah sheet a better tool for people studying together, especially those who join from home, and widen it to the Torah reading, without losing what works: one printed sheet a week that looks the same every week, approved by Daniel by printing it, and the same sheet on the web at haftarah.centralreform.org.

The readers are the Torah from Scratch class at Central Reform Congregation: literate adults, many older, most relying on the English, some reading Hebrew, a few chanting. Some sit at the table with paper, some bring an iPad, some join on Zoom from home, and people look sheets up afterwards. Every feature is judged by one question: does it help these people study the passage together, in the room or remotely?

2.0 means:

- the Torah portion beside the haftarah, as a selection Daniel picks, printed as its own sheet;
- a library that grows year over year, by parashah and by book, where each year's sheet starts from the last one;
- a reader built for studying together: a presenter view for the Zoom leader, links to a verse, copy with citation, Hebrew/English/both, highlights;
- word study (morphology, in-reading concordance), more translations, and "everything Sefaria has on this verse", kept separate from the curated sheet;
- a map and a timeline of the setting, on the web and in print;
- ~~the cantor's recorded chanting with verse sync and trope drills;~~ (struck 2026-10-15: chant and audio are the B'nei Mitzvah portal's product, not this one; see section 10)
- an offline app for iPads;
- the weekly pipeline moved to an MCP server with a short skill, so a whole week can be made from any Claude surface.

Success: the weekly run still ends in one sitting with two PDFs on Drive and the week live on the web; a remote participant can follow the leader's verse, open any source or word; a sheet from 2026 still prints exactly as it printed.

## 2. Settled decisions (Daniel, binding)

**Engine and products.** The text engine in this repo will later serve a multi-tenant B'nei Mitzvah portal, a separate repo (an Overlays-style Next.js app with Postgres). 2.0 is sequenced engine-first. Nothing tenant- or user-specific enters this repo. Engine parameters (customs such as nusach, translation, triennial cycle) are data, not CRC constants. The weekly sheet stays a static product. The printed sheet stays byte-identical for unchanged schema-2 data.

**Audience and scope.** CRC participants, especially remote; the goal is usability for people studying together. Torah portion too. A multi-year library by parashah and by book, each year building on the last. No transliteration. No discussion questions, ever.

**Pipeline.** A hybrid: an MCP server on the haftarah Vercel project plus a short skill, shipped as one plugin. Google sign-in. A private Blob store is the record, Drive its mirror. Delivery only on Daniel's explicit "ship it". A Wednesday cron pre-fetches the coming Shabbat.

**Platform and analytics.** Vercel plan assumed Hobby. Analytics are aggregate-only, through a pluggable provider (Umami Cloud's free tier is the candidate; Daniel creates the account later). Word lookups are counted, never recorded; no identities, ever.

**Torah.** A selection Daniel picks in the briefing when he has asked for a Torah sheet (on request since 2026-10-16, not weekly), with the full Hebcal aliyot list shown. Two PDFs a week (Torah, haftarah), each with its own front page. The same three registers, with a per-reading entry cap. Prior-year entries carry forward by default and Daniel cuts, with a "see also <year>" line. Special weeks carry the Torah reading, with the maftir from Hebcal.

**Torah, ruled 2026-10-16.** Not every week carries a Torah sheet. A Torah sheet is its own product, made only when Daniel asks for one and created and shown the way a haftarah sheet is; the selection stays his. `customs/crc.json` says `rule: "on request"`, and the skill, the runbook and the server's instructions say never to put the Torah pick to him as a step of the week.

**Audio.** Ruled out of this product on 2026-10-15: "fundamentally we are building two different products and we shouldn't merge them. The audio/chanting parts really belong in the other bnei mitzvah project, not here." This repo is the haftarah and Torah study-sheet maker and viewer. The trope engine stays on its branch (`trope-engine-research`) until it moves to the portal; nothing from it is merged here, no audio store, player, rewrite or manifest is built here. If the portal's chant is ever wanted on a study page, it comes back as a later, separate proposal.

**Words.** STEP TAHOT morphology precomputed at publish (OSHB as fallback). Plain-English labels first, the Hebrew grammatical term after. In-reading concordance and frequency. A link out for the whole Tanakh.

**Translations.** A picker with JPS 2023 (default), JPS 1985, Koren, and Metsudah where present, cached at publish. Footnote letters and glosses stay, with a note that they refer to the JPS translation.

**Everything on this verse.** Sefaria's `/api/related`, textual categories only, no user sheets, visibly separate from the curated sheet.

**Map and timeline.** On the web and in print, from `sheet.json` data: Natural Earth plus OpenBible.info. The figures move to the top of page 2 when page 1 cannot hold them. Chronology rounded with "c.", after Galil and the Jewish Study Bible. Empires as text labels. Place names in English only.

**Reader.** A presenter view (`?present`) for a laptop driving Zoom, with keyboard and clicker. Device-local highlights with an export link, no accounts. Teacher highlights published in the week data. An offline installable PWA with an iPad install card. Verse share links. Copy with citation. A Hebrew/English/both toggle.

## 3. The engine and the products

### 3.1 The split

This repo holds three things, kept apart:

| Part | What it is | Where |
|---|---|---|
| **Engine** | Pure, dependency-free code that turns a week's data into a sheet: the data model and its accessors, keys, divine name, trope phrases, the sheet model, the print paginator, the fonts and template | `template/lib.js`, `template/render.js`, `template/*.css`, `template/sheet.html`, `template/fonts/`, `template/voices.json` |
| **Products** | CRC's weekly sheet: the printed PDFs and the static site | `scripts/build.mjs`, `scripts/check.mjs`, `site/`, `vercel.json` |
| **Pipeline** | How a week is made and delivered: fetch, digest, lint, build, deliver, publish; later the MCP server and the skill | `scripts/*.mjs`, `api/` (Wave 6), the plugin |

The portal will consume the engine as a package (proved in the Wave 6 spike) and may read published week data from the public Blob store (`index.json`, `weeks/<slug>/sheet.json` and the side files), read-only. Schema 3 and index v2 (section 4) are that interface: versioned, additive, documented in README.md.

### 3.2 Customs are data

Every choice a congregation might make differently is a field in a customs record, not a constant in code. CRC's record is `customs/crc.json`, introduced in Wave 2 when fetch first needs it; the engine and fetch take a customs object as a parameter.

```jsonc
{
  "id": "crc",
  "calendar": { "diaspora": true },                 // Hebcal i=off
  "haftarahRite": "ashkenazi",                      // today's DEFAULT_HAFTAROT in fetch.mjs
  "torah": { "mode": "selection", "triennial": null },  // CRC: a selection, on request (ruled 2026-10-16)
  "nusach": "eastern-ashkenazi",                    // the cantor's chant tradition; open item 11.1
  "versions": { "he": "Miqra according to the Masorah", "en": "THE JPS TANAKH: Gender-Sensitive Edition" },
  "translations": ["jps2023", "jps1985", "koren", "metsudah"],
  "divineName": "yy",                               // יי with the verse's accent on the second yud
  "entryCap": 24,                                   // per reading; a lint warning above it
  "series": { "name": "Torah from Scratch" }
}
```

### 3.3 What never enters this repo

Tenants, users, accounts, student names or progress, B'nei Mitzvah schedules, learners' recordings, any other congregation's customs, credentials, and week data (`weeks/`, JPS text). The repo stays public and the weekly sheet stays a static product: no database, no server-rendered pages, no reader accounts.

## 4. The reading data model

### 4.1 Schema 3: `readings[]`

A week may carry more than one reading. Schema 3 moves what was `haftarah` plus its `verses`, `glosses` and `commentary` into one element of `readings[]`; everything that belongs to the week (dates, opening note, glossary, next week, credits) stays top-level. torah-and-library.md section 4.1 argues the shape; codebase-readiness.md section 0 lists every place the one-reading assumption lives.

```jsonc
{
  "schema": 3,
  "slug": "2026-10-17-noach",
  "shabbat": { "civil": "2026-10-17", "civilDisplay": "October 17, 2026", "hebrew": "…", "hebrewEn": "6 Cheshvan 5787",
               "parashah": { "en": "Noach", "he": "נֹחַ", "ref": "Genesis 6:9-11:32" }, "special": null },
  "series": { "name": "Torah from Scratch", "year": "5787" },
  "readings": [
    { "id": "torah", "kind": "torah",                                   // Wave 2
      "ref": "Genesis 6:9-7:16", "book": "Genesis", "selection": "aliyot 1-2",
      "aliyot": [ { "n": 1, "ref": "Genesis 6:9-22" }, { "n": "M", "ref": "Genesis 11:29-32" } ],   // the full Hebcal list
      "incipit": {}, "versions": {}, "verses": [], "glosses": [], "commentary": [] },
    { "id": "haftarah", "kind": "haftarah",
      "ref": "Isaiah 54:1-55:5", "heRef": "…", "book": "Isaiah", "title": { "en": "Noach" },
      "incipit": { "he": "…", "en": "…" }, "versions": { "he": "…", "en": "…" }, "defaultHaftarah": null,
      "verses": [ { "book": "Isaiah", "chapter": 54, "verse": 1, "he": "<html>", "en": "<html>", "notes": [], "break": null } ],
      "glosses": [ { "verse": 1, "chapter": 54, "lemma": "…", "en": "…", "text": "…" } ],
      "commentary": [ { "id": "e-3d5f2921", "verse": 1, "register": "traditional", "source": "Rashi", "sourceRef": "Rashi on Isaiah 54:1", "text": "…", "order": 1 } ] }
  ],
  "openingNote": { "calendar": "…", "setting": ["…"] },
  "glossary": [], "nextWeek": null, "credits": {},
  "highlights": [ { "reading": "haftarah", "chapter": 54, "verse": 3, "words": [2, 4], "note": "<html>" } ],
  "places": [], "timeline": []                                           // Wave 4
}
```

Rules:

- Reading ids are lowercase letters, unique in the sheet: `haftarah`, `torah`. `kind` is `haftarah` or `torah`.
- Every verse names its `book` (verse numbers repeat across books; a Torah selection and a haftarah can share chapter and verse numbers).
- The key series, the trope final cadence, the Sefaria refs and the chapter-less entry rule work per reading, exactly as they work for the one reading of schema 2.
- Schema-2 files stay valid forever. Published Blob copies are never rewritten; `migrate.mjs --to 3` converts a working file when Daniel wants it, and fetch writes schema 3 from Wave 2.

### 4.2 The back-compatible accessors (`template/lib.js`)

- `readings(data)`: the list of readings. A schema-2 file comes back as one reading `{ id: "haftarah", kind: "haftarah", ...haftarah, verses, glosses, commentary }`.
- `primaryReading(data)`: the haftarah, else the first reading. The week is known by it: index row, PDF name, page title.
- `readingView(data, id)`: one reading in the schema-2 shape, for the print paginator and every script that reads one reading (build, check, lint, deliver, the web page's `sheetModel`). A schema-2 file comes back as the same object, untouched, which is what makes the print guarantee (section 6) cheap to hold. For schema 3 the view strips `verses[].book` and `commentary[].id`, which the print never read, and shares the glosses array with the file so `lint --fix` writes through it.

### 4.3 Stable identity

| Thing | Id | Given by | Used by |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reading | `haftarah`, `torah` | fetch / migrate | row ids, highlights, words.json |
| Verse | `<reading>-<chapter>-<verse>`, e.g. `haftarah-54-3` | derived | page anchors and share links, presenter state, highlights, analytics |
| Commentary entry | `e-` + 8 hex digits (FNV-1a of reading, chapter, verse, source, sourceRef, lemma at the moment it is first given), `-2`, `-3` on a collision | migrate 2→3 now; fetch, carry-forward and the MCP `save_entries` later | prior-year reuse, "see also", dedupe, the library |
| Word | the nth Hebrew word of a verse as the web page counts them (`.w` spans; footnote letters and the ketiv are not words) | derived | highlights, words.json (`i`) |

The FNV-1a hash is computed over the UTF-16 code units of the joined string (`charCodeAt`), not its UTF-8 bytes, so any other producer of ids (the MCP, a script in another language) must hash the same way. An id is given once and kept when the entry is edited; it is never recomputed from changed content. The first release's verse anchors (`#v54-3`) keep working: the page maps them to the primary reading and rewrites the address.

### 4.4 `highlights[]` (teacher highlights)

Daniel's marks for discussion, published with the week: `{ reading, chapter, verse, words: [first, last] | null, note: "<html>" | null }`. `words` are zero-based word indexes (section 4.3), inclusive; a range outside the verse is clamped, a reversed one ignored. On the web a highlighted verse gets a rubric bar and a wash, the words a tint, and the note sits in the verse's apparatus. They are web-only: the printed sheet does not show them. Device-local highlights (a reader's own, Wave 5) never enter the week data.

### 4.5 `places[]` and `timeline[]` (Wave 4)

From maps-timelines.md section 5, adjusted to Daniel's rulings (English names only; empires as text labels; rounded "c." dates):

```jsonc
"places": [ { "id": "gibeah", "name": "Gibeah", "role": "scene | context | empire", "lat": 31.82, "lon": 35.24,
              "source": "openbible:<id>", "label": "r | l | t | b", "approved": false } ],
"mapRegion": "land | near-east | [lonMin, latMin, lonMax, latMax]",     // optional; default from the places
"timeline": [ { "id": "david", "label": "David", "kind": "king | empire | prophet | composition | event",
                "chronology": "david" } ,                                 // dates from data/chronology.json
              { "id": "exile", "label": "Exile to Babylon", "kind": "event", "start": -587, "end": -538, "approx": false, "works": ["…"] } ]
```

`lat`/`lon` are filled from the committed gazetteer (OpenBible confidence 500 or higher); a hand-entered coordinate needs `approved: true`. A `role: "empire"` place is a text label at an anchor point, never a shaded area. King, empire and prophet items cite a `data/chronology.json` key; event and composition items name `works` from the bibliography. The build refuses a place it cannot resolve and names it, as it refuses a citation.

### 4.6 `index.json` v2

Additive: every v1 field stays, so the current landing page keeps working through the rollout. Per week, added in Wave 1:

```jsonc
{ "parashah": "Noach", "parashahHe": "נֹחַ", "parashahKey": "noach", "cycle": "5787", "special": null,
  "readings": [ { "id": "haftarah", "kind": "haftarah", "ref": "Isaiah 54:1–55:5", "book": "Isaiah" } ] }
```

The file says `"schema": 2`. Wave 2 adds `parashahKeys` (both halves of a combined parashah), `tags` (special Shabbatot), `hasWords`, `hasLinks`, the derived `library` block (torah-and-library.md section 4.3), and a one-off `rebuild-index` command that backfills the v1 rows from the published sheets. Ten years of weeks stay under 200 KB, so the index is never sharded.

### 4.7 Side files

Each sits in the public Blob store beside `sheet.json`, is written by publish, is fetched by the page only when first needed, and is listed in the index (`has*`) so the page never asks for one that does not exist.

| File | Wave | Contents | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| `weeks/<slug>/words.json` | 3 | per reading and verse, each word as the page counts it (`verseWords`): its morphemes (`seg[]` with the decoded fields, STEP's gloss, the dStrong), its dictionary word (`lem`), the code and a ketiv; a `lemmas` table with TBESH's gloss and the whole-Tanakh count (`all`); the in-reading count is derived on the page | STEP TAHOT, OSHB fallback |
| `weeks/<slug>/translations.json` | 3 | per reading, per offered version, verse-aligned HTML with `versionTitle` and `license` | Sefaria v3, cached at publish |
| `weeks/<slug>/links.json` | 3 | per verse, the related links in textual categories, grouped by category and work: `{ category, works: [{ title, refs, he }] }` (`he` lists the refs with no English); `heRef` is not stored (size: 60 to 260 links a verse; the Hebrew title shows when an item is opened) | Sefaria `/api/related`, at publish |

Amended 2026-10-15 (Wave 3): the published `sheet.json` carries a `side` manifest written by publish, `{ words, translations: { <reading>: [{ key, label }] }, links }` (`words` holds the source, `tahot` or `oshb`, not a boolean; `links` is `true`), so the page never asks for a side file that is not there; the index rows carry `hasWords`, `hasLinks` and `translations`.

### 4.8 What publish sends

`publish.mjs` sends only the top-level fields on its whitelist, and README.md's "Data file" section documents exactly that list; a test holds the two together, so a new field can never vanish from the web silently (codebase-readiness.md section 2). Cleaning works per reading: rejected entries and glosses are removed from every reading, rejected glossary items and highlights from the week, and every `status` key from everything.

## 5. Feature areas

Each area lists the decisions it carries and the acceptance criteria its wave must meet.

### 5.1 Foundations and hygiene (Wave 1)

Pays the debt codebase-readiness.md section 5 ranks first, so every later change is safe.

- **Print golden.** See section 6.
- **Browser checks in `npm test`.** `check.mjs` on both fixtures and `web-check.mjs` run inside `npm test`, skipped with a message when no Chrome or Edge is present. Every page the checks read from headless Chrome comes through one helper with a kill timer and with UTF-8 decoded once (a stdout chunk can end inside a Hebrew letter; today's helpers decode per chunk).
- **Test isolation.** `tests/helpers/isolatedEnv.mjs` builds a child's environment from an allow-list, with HOME and USERPROFILE in a temp folder and HAFTARAH_DROPBOX_CONFIG and HAFTARAH_WEB_CONFIG pointing at missing files; HAFTARAH_BLOB_TOKEN and the drop-box variables are absent. A lint test fails any test file that spawns a process without it (the 2026-10-14 delivery hazard).
- **Content-Security-Policy.** The inline boot scripts in `site/*.html` become modules; the one inline `style` attribute goes to CSS; `vercel.json` sends, on every path except `/data/` (a policy on a top-level PDF can stop the browser's PDF viewer), `default-src 'self'; script-src 'self'; style-src 'self'; img-src 'self'; font-src 'self'; connect-src 'self' https://www.sefaria.org; object-src 'none'; base-uri 'self'; form-action 'self'; frame-ancestors 'none'`. Later waves widen it deliberately (analytics origin, `media-src` for audio, `worker-src` for the PWA), each with a test.
- **Accessibility.** Note popovers and source buttons name what they control (`aria-controls`); Hebrew words are one tab stop per verse with arrow keys inside it (Left reads on, since the text runs right to left); the settings dialog keeps focus inside while open; the closed side panel is `inert`.
- **Storage.** Device preferences live under `haftarah:v1:` keys; the three keys of the first release (`size`, `theme`, `trope`) move there once.
- **One contract.** The week-slug pattern and the data paths live in one module (`site/app/contract.js`) that publish, web-check and the page import; a test keeps `vercel.json`'s rewrite in step.

Acceptance: `npm test` passes with and without Chrome on the machine; no test file can reach a real credential; the deployed pages report no CSP violation; a keyboard user crosses a 25-verse reading in about 25 Tab presses, not 400.

### 5.2 The reader (Wave 1, Wave 5)

reader-features.md sections 1 to 3; peer-scan.md section 3 for why these and not others.

- **Presenter view** (`?present`, Wave 1). One verse at a time, large, for a laptop sharing its screen: the verse label and "3 / 22" top left, the Hebrew, the English, the verse's commentary on `c`, a next-verse preview on `n`, black screen on `b` or `.`, trope colors on `t`, size on `+`/`−`, exit on Escape. Next on Right, Down, Space, Enter and PageDown (what presenter remotes send); previous on Left, Up, PageUp and Backspace; Home and End. Keys with Ctrl, Cmd or Alt are left to the browser; Space and Enter on a focused button press the button. A fixed light, high-contrast palette independent of the reader's theme; trope colors start off. The type fits the screen: a long verse shrinks, it never scrolls. The current verse is in the address (`?present#haftarah-54-3`), so a reload resumes and the leader can paste the link. The screen stays awake where the browser allows. Entry: a "Present on a shared screen" button in the Aa card, which starts at the verse on screen.
- **Verse links** (Wave 1). Every verse has an address, `/<slug>#haftarah-54-3`. Opening one scrolls to the verse and marks it. "Copy link" sits in each verse's header in the apparatus; on a phone, tapping the number of a verse without commentary opens just that header.
- **Copy with citation** (Wave 1). "Copy verse" puts the verse on the clipboard in the reader's language setting, then a line "— Isaiah 54:3 (JPS 2023)" and the verse link: sized for a Zoom chat.
- **Hebrew / Both / English** (Wave 1). A three-way switch in the Aa card, remembered per device.
- **Teacher highlights** (Wave 1). Section 4.4.
- **Device-local highlights** (Wave 5, with the PWA, because Safari clears script storage after 7 days of use unless the site is installed; reader-features.md section 3). A long-press on the verse number, or "Mark this verse" in its header, marks it; "My marks" lists them; export as a link (`#m=54-3,54-9`, a fragment that never reaches a server) and as text (shipped in Wave 5; the link is `#m=haftarah-54-3,haftarah-54-9`). `navigator.storage.persist()` is requested. Never synced, never sent.

Acceptance: a leader with a presenter remote can step a 25-verse haftarah on Zoom without touching the laptop; a participant who opens a pasted verse link lands on that verse; copy output is plain text that pastes cleanly into Zoom chat; every control has a keyboard path and a 44px touch target.

### 5.3 Analytics (Wave 1)

reader-features.md section 5. On Hobby, Vercel Web Analytics has no custom events, so the provider is pluggable and Umami Cloud's free tier is the candidate.

- The page calls one hook, `track(name, props)`. The provider is chosen by a committed build-time module (`site/app/analytics-config.js`); it ships as `none`, which sends nothing.
- Events and the only properties each may carry: `verse_view` {week, verse} once per page view per verse; `source_open` {week, source}; `word_lookup` {week}, a count, never the word; `present_start` {week}; `install` {}. Anything else is dropped by the hook, not by the provider.
- No identifiers, cookies or storage for analytics. Do Not Track or Global Privacy Control turns it off entirely. The URL sent is the path only, never the query or the verse fragment.
- Turning Umami on is one commit: the config (website id and endpoint) and the endpoint's origin in the CSP's `connect-src`. A test refuses one without the other. When it goes on, the footer gains one line, drafted as "We count visits and which sources are opened; nothing identifies you. Opening a source or a word asks Sefaria for it." and shown to Daniel before it ships.

Acceptance: with the shipped config, a reader's browser makes no request beyond the site and Sefaria; with Umami configured, a word lookup sends exactly `{ "week": "<slug>" }`.

### 5.4 Torah portion and the library (Wave 2)

torah-and-library.md.

- **Calendar and selection.** `fetch.mjs --torah` reads Hebcal's leyning item for the week: the full kriyah (seven aliyot and maftir), the triennial third, and on special Shabbatot the special maftir, which always comes from Hebcal. The briefing shows the full aliyot list; Daniel picks the selection (`--torah "Genesis 6:9-7:16"` or `--torah-pick aliyah:1-2`). Fetch reads by selection, never by parashah, and writes `texts-torah.json` and `texts-haftarah.json`, each held near 2 MB, with a Torah core list of commentators and a skip list for super-commentaries and quoting commentary. Special weeks and festivals carry their Torah reading.
- **Two PDFs.** `build.mjs --reading torah|haftarah` writes `sheet-torah.pdf` and `sheet.pdf` from one `sheet.json`, each with its own front page. The paginator learns the reading kind from the build options (Torah ends on a plain sof pasuk; the running head says Torah), never from data the haftarah build already reads, so the haftarah build's options and output are unchanged. Deliver and publish send both; the Drive names gain "Torah" and "Haftarah".
- **Entry cap.** The same three registers. Lint warns above `customs.entryCap` entries per reading (24 to start).
- **Carry forward.** At fetch, earlier-cycle weeks with the same `parashahKey` and kind are read from the public index (Drive only when rejections matter). Their printed entries whose verses fall inside this year's selection are copied in with their ids and `carried: "<slug>"`; they print unless Daniel cuts them. The briefing gains a "Last time" section (what was printed, what is new in this year's range). The calendar paragraph of the opening note ends with a proposed sentence pointing to the earlier sheet ("See also the 5786 sheet: haftarah.centralreform.org/5786/noach."), so it prints through the existing template with no template change; the web page shows the same link in the hero.
- **Library.** The landing becomes four doors over one list: by week (default; two lines per row when a week has a Torah reading), by parashah (a 54-cell grid in Torah order, five book bands, a dot per year, empty cells muted), by book (haftarah books Joshua to Malachi with chapters covered, and the Torah books), and special Shabbatot. `/noach` (latest sheet for that parashah) and `/5787/noach` resolve in a `library.html` shell from the index and replace the address with the canonical `/<date-slug>`, which stays the permanent page. `vercel.json` routes these with an explicit allow-list generated from a committed 54-row `site/app/parashot.js`; a test keeps the two in step.

Acceptance: a Torah week produces two PDFs with front pages, both on Drive and the web; the haftarah PDF of a week is identical whether or not the week also has a Torah reading; `/noach` opens the latest Noach sheet; a 5788 Noach fetch shows "Last time" and carries 5787's printed entries in range.

### 5.5 Words (Wave 3)

words-and-translations.md sections 1, 2 and 5.

- `scripts/words.mjs`, run by publish, reads the four STEP TAHOT files and TBESH cached outside the repo (`~/.haftarah-cache/`), aligns them to the page's words per verse by longest common subsequence on consonant skeletons (after removing MAM's `{ס}`/`{פ}` and splitting on maqaf and paseq), and writes `words.json`. A word that does not align gets no morphology and is listed in publish's output; nothing is guessed. A verse that comes back empty fails the publish. OSHB morphhb is the fallback source with the same output shape.
- The word panel leads with a plain-English sentence and gives the Hebrew term after it: "A verb, past tense (completed action): stretched out. Simple action (Qal)." ### 5.10 The offline app (Wave 5)

reader-features.md section 4. A manifest and icons from the mural; a hand-written service worker (precache the shell, fonts and app files under a build-hash cache name that web-build writes; week JSON stale-while-revalidate; Sefaria answers cached on view, bounded); an "Update available" note applied on the next launch, never mid-session or mid-presentation; a two-step "Add to Home Screen" card shown only in iPad and iPhone Safari outside the installed app. CSP gains `worker-src 'self'` and `manifest-src 'self'`. Shipped 2026-10-15 (Wave 5): `site/sw.js` is a module worker over the pure functions in `site/app/offline.js`, registered from every page with `updateViaCache: "none"`. It keeps three caches. `haftarah-shell-<build>` holds the precached shell (every shipped file but the worker, the licence texts and the unused `EzraSIL-SR.ttf`) and is replaced whole on a new build; `web-build` writes the build, the first 12 hex characters of a sha256 over every other output file's path and bytes, and the precache list into the worker, so the name changes exactly when a file does. `haftarah-data` holds the week files and the index, at most 60 entries; a week file is answered from the cache and refreshed behind it (the week a first visit opened is asked for again once the worker takes control, so it is cached too). The index and Sefaria's answers are network-first, hurried to the cached copy after 3 seconds only when one exists (the slow answer still fills the cache behind it); with nothing cached they wait for the network. `haftarah-sefaria` holds Sefaria's answers, at most 100, oldest dropped first. PDFs and any request but GET are never cached, and a navigation under `/data/` is left to the browser. A navigation to a path with a shell (the landing, a week, a library alias) is answered from the current build's shell cache, the network asked only on a miss and its answer not cached, so an open tab that reloads after a deploy keeps its old build whole until the next launch; an unknown path is network-first with the 3-second timeout, then the cached 404 page. The worker never calls `skipWaiting`: a new build installs and waits for the next launch. The page then shows the update note, "This site has been updated. The new version opens the next time you launch it." with Dismiss, a status strip that never appears on a first install or in the presenter. `site/manifest.webmanifest` and four icons rendered once from the mural by `tools/make-icons.mjs` (192, 512, a maskable 512, an apple-touch 180) make the site installable. The install card appears only in iPad and iPhone Safari outside the installed app, on the week page and the landing, its note reads "Sheets you open from the Home Screen stay readable without a connection, and marks made there are kept." (iOS keeps a Home Screen app's storage apart from Safari's), and "Not now" dismisses it for good. A reader's marks stay under `haftarah:v1:marks:<slug>`; Mark this verse in a verse's header (its label alone carries the state) or a long press marks one, and `navigator.storage.persist()` is asked after the first mark is written. The link is `/<slug>#m=haftarah-54-3,haftarah-54-9`, carrying the reading; a bare `54-9` means the primary reading, and what is not a verse on the page is dropped. A link opened with marks in it shows a strip offering them ("N marked verses shared with you." and Add to my marks); a store that refuses a write says "This mark could not be saved on this device." The policy gains `worker-src 'self'` and `manifest-src 'self'`. The browser check registers the worker against its own server and holds Chrome's virtual clock with small real requests to `/sw.js?wait=N` while the worker settles (check mode only).

Acceptance: an installed iPad opens last week's sheet with no network; a republished week shows its new text on the next launch.

## 6. The print-identity guarantee

"The printed sheet must stay byte-identical for unchanged schema-2 data." What prints is `sheet.html` (written by `build.mjs` from `sheet.json` and `template/sheet.html`) rendered by Chrome with `render.js`, `lib.js`, `sheet.css` and the fonts. The golden test guards each layer, starting before any 2.0 code lands (Wave 1, first task):

1. **The built HTML.** For each fixture, `build.mjs --no-pdf --trope` runs and the sha256 of `sheet.html` and `sheet-trope.html` (line endings folded to `\n`, since Windows checkouts carry CRLF) must equal the recorded value. Node only; always runs.
2. **The rendered page.** For each fixture, Chrome renders both files and the sha256 of the `<html>` tag render.js stamps (pages, keys, divine-name count) plus the whole `<main id="sheet">` must equal the recorded value. Checked when the recording's browser version and platform match the machine's; otherwise skipped with a message naming both, because a Chrome update may move a pixel. Re-recording (`node scripts/golden.mjs --update`) is a deliberate step after looking at the PDFs.
3. **Schema 3 prints the same.** Each fixture migrated to schema 3 and built at the same folder depth gives a `sheet.html` that differs from the schema-2 one only inside the embedded data, and that data equals the schema-2 file; rendered, it gives the same page signature as the schema-2 build.

Rules that follow: render.js, sheet.css and template/sheet.html change only in Wave 4 (and in Wave 2 only behind a build option the haftarah build does not pass); every new data field is optional and ignored by the print; any change to `lib.js` functions the print calls keeps the golden green. In Wave 4 the golden is re-recorded once, in the commit that adds the figures, and only for a new fixture that carries places and a timeline; the existing fixtures (three by Wave 4: week-min, week-notes, week-torah) keep their recorded values, which proves sheets without figures did not change.

Note, 2026-10-15 (Wave 4): the golden's sources layer was re-recorded in the commit that added the figures (2efffe4), the three existing fixtures proven unmoved, and `week-figures` (I Samuel 20, with places and a timeline) added as the fourth fixture. It was re-recorded once more in that task's fix round (1fbd54e), from the same pre-task golden file, when the labels reached the 9pt floor and the page-2 map was narrowed to 2.6in; the three old fixtures again did not move and `week-figures` was added again. Together the two commits are the one re-record the plan allowed. The fixtures prove less than "every sheet without figures is unmoved": that fix round's end-matter placement in render.js (the inline end matter now starts below margin notes that run lower than the last verse) also moves a sheet without figures whose margin notes run below its last verse, for the better (on 2026-10-10-bereshit-machar-chodesh the end matter moves down to clear a note it used to print over); the three golden fixtures have no such page, so they did not move.

## 7. Privacy
